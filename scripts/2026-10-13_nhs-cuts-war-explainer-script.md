# Why is the NHS being cut to pay for war?

## Part 1. The hook (0:00–1:10)

**[VISUAL]** Exterior of Medway Maritime Hospital, Gillingham. Caption: “Medway Maritime Hospital, Kent.”

**[NARRATION]**
Medway Maritime Hospital in Gillingham serves more than 400,000 people across Medway and Swale. Its managers have now been told to cut 861 posts over five years. That is almost one in seven of the hospital’s workforce. They must also find £143.8 million in savings, about a quarter of everything the hospital spends.

**[VISUAL]** Split screen. Left: “Medway: £143.8 million over five years.” Right: “Nuclear weapons: more than £63 billion over four years.”

**[NARRATION]**
At the end of June, the government published its Defence Investment Plan. It sets aside more than £63 billion over four years for nuclear weapons alone. At that rate, Medway’s entire five-year savings target would cover less than four days of Britain’s nuclear programme.

**[VISUAL]** World map. Caption: “Humanitarian aid: $47.5 billion (2022) → $33.3 billion (2025).”

**[NARRATION]**
On the same day it reported the Kent cuts, the World Socialist Web Site reported a second story. Between 2022 and 2025, humanitarian aid from the major powers fell by 30 percent. Last year, 64 million fewer people received help.

A hospital in Kent and a feeding centre in Zimbabwe look like separate stories. This video sets out why they have the same cause.

## Part 2. What is happening in Britain (1:10–2:40)

**[VISUAL]** Animated bar chart of planned NHS job cuts by region: South East 5,152; London 4,232; North West 3,701.

**[NARRATION]**
Medway is the sharp end of a national policy. Kent and Medway recorded the largest NHS deficit in England last year. Hospitals that fail to balance their books now face financial penalties. The union UNISON found that trusts plan to cut at least 21,663 NHS posts by 2028, and possibly 30,000, including nurses, midwives and healthcare assistants.

This is happening while the waiting list stands at 7.33 million, and while long delays in A&E were linked to more than 300 deaths a week in 2025.

**[VISUAL]** Text build: “Defence Investment Plan: £298 billion to 2029/30.” Then: “Health capital budget: −£570 million. Up to 18 hospital building projects delayed or scrapped.”

**[NARRATION]**
The money is going somewhere. The Defence Investment Plan takes military spending to £298 billion over four years. To help pay for it, every government department had to hand over part of its building budget. Health lost £570 million, putting up to 18 hospital building projects at risk, with the NHS repair backlog already close to £16 billion.

Andy Burnham promised to fund the plan in full. He has also committed Britain to NATO’s target of 5 percent of national income for military and related spending by 2035. The Resolution Foundation puts the extra cost at £25 billion every year. The *Times* told Burnham directly to cut pensions and welfare to pay for it.

UNISON’s leaders have published the figures and organised no opposition. They promise to resist compulsory redundancies while jobs disappear through vacancy freezes and “voluntary” schemes.

## Part 3. The same pattern across NATO (2:40–4:10)

**[VISUAL]** Map of Europe and North America. Germany and the United States highlighted in turn.

**[NARRATION]**
Britain is one case among many.

In Germany, military spending is set to more than double in five years, from €82 billion in 2025 to nearly €180 billion by 2030. In one week this month, parliament approved four more warships, bringing a new frigate order to €11.6 billion.

**[VISUAL]** Split screen. Left: “Eight frigates: €11.6 billion.” Right: “Care reform savings target: up to €8 billion.”

**[NARRATION]**
The same government has passed a law to save up to €8 billion on care for the elderly. It removes all paid support for people at the lowest care level and tightens the rules so that fewer people qualify for help at all. A place in a nursing home already costs families around €3,500 a month.

In the United States, the Trump administration has asked Congress for $1.5 trillion for the military, an increase of 42 percent. The same budget cuts $73 billion from everything else, including an eighth of the health department’s budget. That follows roughly $1 trillion in Medicaid cuts over ten years, passed in 2025. Trump himself has said that the federal government must look after one thing, “military protection.”

**[VISUAL]** Global figure: “World military spending: $2.9 trillion, a record.”

**[NARRATION]**
Across the world, military spending has reached a record $2.9 trillion. The European powers together now spend $864 billion. Britain has given Ukraine £357 million in humanitarian aid since the war began, alongside £13.7 billion in military support.

These governments describe themselves in very different ways. Labour in Britain, the Christian Democrats and Social Democrats in Germany, and Trump’s Republicans in Washington are all doing the same thing. When governments of every colour move in one direction, the cause lies in the system they all serve.

## Part 4. The explanation (4:10–6:10)

**[VISUAL]** Archive image of Lenin. Title card: “V. I. Lenin, *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism*, 1916.”

**[NARRATION]**
In 1916, in the middle of the First World War, the Russian revolutionary Lenin wrote a short book called *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism*. He set out to explain why the most advanced countries in the world had turned on each other.

His answer was that capitalism had changed. Giant monopolies and banks now dominated each national economy. They needed markets, raw materials and places to invest across the whole globe. And by the early twentieth century, the world had already been divided up among a handful of great powers.

**[VISUAL]** Full-screen quotation.

> “Imperialism is capitalism in that stage of development in which the dominance of monopolies and finance capital has established itself … in which the division of all territories of the globe among the great capitalist powers has been completed.”
> *V. I. Lenin, 1916*

**[NARRATION]**
Once the world is divided, one power can gain only at another’s expense. War becomes the means of redividing it. Lenin showed that war grows out of the normal workings of capitalism in this stage.

That analysis describes our own time. The United States, its economic dominance in decline, is using military force to hold its position, from Iran to Ukraine and in its preparations against China. The European powers are rearming to secure their own share. The World Socialist Web Site has described this as a new redivision of the world.

**[VISUAL]** Graphic: arrow from “Arms budgets” to “Cuts to hospitals, care, pensions, welfare.”

**[NARRATION]**
Why must workers at home pay for it? Weapons cost money, and all new wealth is produced by the labour of the working class. The ruling class has no intention of paying from its own fortunes. In Germany, the growth in wealth of 5,000 super-rich individuals in a single year could have covered the entire cost of long-term care six times over. Governments chose instead to cut care.

War also requires discipline at home. Lenin wrote that imperialism brings “political reaction all along the line.” Wages must be held down, protests contained and opposition silenced. That is why the trade union leaderships are drawn ever closer to governments, and why the police were sent against schoolchildren protesting in France this month.

The NHS was won in 1948, when the ruling class feared a radicalised working class returning from war. To the ruling class today it is a cost standing between them and the arms budget.

**[VISUAL]** Full-screen quotation.

> “The main enemy is at home!”
> *Karl Liebknecht, 1915*

**[NARRATION]**
In 1915, the German socialist Karl Liebknecht summed it up. The enemy of German workers was the German ruling class that had sent them to war. The same holds for British workers today.

## Part 5. The 1930s (6:10–7:40)

**[VISUAL]** Archive footage of 1930s dole queues and hunger marches.

**[NARRATION]**
This has happened before. In 1931, during the Great Depression, the banks demanded cuts to unemployment pay. The Labour government of Ramsay MacDonald split. MacDonald formed a National Government with the Conservatives, cut benefits by 10 percent and brought in the hated household means test, which forced families to sell their possessions before they could get relief.

A few years later, the same governments found hundreds of millions of pounds for rearmament, raising loans for weapons while the means test went on punishing the unemployed.

**[VISUAL]** Archive footage of Nazi Germany, 1933 to 1936. Content note on screen.

**[NARRATION]**
In Germany, the Nazis destroyed the trade unions in May 1933, froze wages and poured the nation’s resources into arms. Their leaders told workers openly that guns came before butter.

Both paths ended in the Second World War, which killed tens of millions of people.

**[VISUAL]** Full-screen quotation.

> “The world political situation as a whole is chiefly characterized by a historical crisis of the leadership of the proletariat.”
> *Leon Trotsky, 1938*

**[NARRATION]**
In the 1930s, workers in Britain, Germany, France and Spain had immense power. Leon Trotsky explained that what they lacked was leadership. The Labour and Social Democratic parties, and the Stalinist Communist parties, tied workers to their own national governments. Workers lost the chance to stop the slide into war. That is the central lesson for today.

## Part 6. Socialism or barbarism (7:40–9:10)

**[VISUAL]** Full-screen quotation.

> “Bourgeois society stands at the crossroads, either transition to Socialism or regression into Barbarism.”
> *Rosa Luxemburg, 1915*

**[NARRATION]**
Rosa Luxemburg wrote these words from a prison cell during the First World War. She posed the choice facing humanity in its starkest form.

The signs of barbarism are all around us. Aid workers killed in Gaza. Governments threatening nuclear war over Ukraine. Hospitals cut while warship orders grow.

**[VISUAL]** Footage of recent protests: France, Spain, Belgium, Germany.

**[NARRATION]**
The other side of the crossroads is also visible. This month, nearly half a million young people and workers marched in France against austerity and militarism. Mass housing protests in Spain are calling for a general strike. School students in Belgium and Germany are striking against cuts and conscription. In Germany, 175,000 workers protested against the government’s social cuts.

Workers in every country face the same governments, the same arms budgets and the same cuts. They need a movement of their own, independent of the trade union bureaucracies and the parties that serve the ruling class. In hospitals that means rank-and-file committees, run by staff and patients, to oppose every job cut and every closure, linked with workers across Europe and internationally.

And it means a socialist programme. The wealth of society, now poured into weapons and into the fortunes of a tiny few, must be placed under the democratic control of the working class and used to meet human need.

**[VISUAL]** End card: “World Socialist Web Site: wsws.org. NHS FightBack. Socialist Equality Party.”

**[NARRATION]**
To read the reporting behind this video, and to join the fight against cuts and war, visit the World Socialist Web Site at wsws.org.
